Alternatives tested

What the evidence says

  1. Fill fell in only two metros, and they hold half the unfilled shifts.

    Philadelphia went from 84% to 73% and Baltimore from 83% to 71%. Every other established metro improved. Q4.1 · Q4.3

  2. Demand grew everywhere. The difference is how many shifts each AT took.

    Other metros met 44–54% more requests by having each AT fill more shifts. In Philadelphia that number didn't move, and in Baltimore it rose least while requests grew fastest. Q4.2

  3. The same two metros are losing contract customers.

    25% retention against 82% elsewhere: 6 of the 8 lost contracts and $460k of the $500k churned value. Q4.5 · Q4.11A

  4. Segment, contract load and sales don't explain the concentration.

    Churn there spans every segment Q4.6, the metro with the heaviest contract load still fills 80% Q4.12, and weak outbound sales are a separate issue Q4.8.

  5. The problems sit in the same place, but cause isn't proven.

    Fill before renewal was only 1.5 points lower for churned contracts, and most churn came before the September drop. The exit reasons are still needed. Q4.4

  6. More is at risk, and the 70% plan now needs every renewal.

    5 renewals worth $387k are due there by December. All 8 company renewals must go through for 2026 to reach 70%. Q4.7 · Q4.11B

Part 4 · What to do

Recommendations

Keep the 90-day focus on Philadelphia and Baltimore. They account for nearly half of September's unfilled shifts, 92% of churned annual contract value ($460k of $500k) and $387k of renewals due by December. Each action below says what to do, what to verify first, and when to commit resources.

  1. 60 days · diagnose first 2 weeks

    Fulfillment program in Philadelphia and Baltimore

    Start with a two-week diagnosis. Little or no growth in shifts filled per AT can come from availability, scheduling, travel, pay or competing contract assignments. Test the cause at shift level, then pilot the matching fix.

    Target 75% fill in each metro first. Treat 80% as a stretch goal, subject to cost and feasibility. Track filled shifts, customer coverage and incremental cost weekly.

    Because fill fell only here, these metros hold half the unfilled shifts, and their ATs took on few or no extra shifts while requests grew (Philadelphia 4.20 → 4.23, Baltimore 3.97 → 4.23). Q4.1 · Q4.2 · Q4.3

  2. Oct–Dec

    An owner and a dated plan for each of the 5 priority renewals ($387k)

    Give each account an owner, a verified renewal status, a coverage review and a dated next customer milestone. At the 09/30 snapshot the two October accounts come first by deadline. Start the two December accounts early too: they are $232k, about 60% of the exposure.

    Standard outreach for the other three renewals

    Potomac Indoor Center, Washington DC ($112k); Charlotte Indoor Sports ($96k); Lehigh Valley Volleyball ($20k). Verify each account's own coverage before deciding whether extra support is needed.

  4. Every new deal

    Capacity approval for each new commitment

    Marketplace Operations and Contract Services check AT availability for the proposed shifts, scheduling feasibility, expected margin and the effect on existing obligations. Approve deals that pass. Delay, reprice or revise those with unresolved coverage or economics. Monitor sustained metro fill alongside the deal review.

    Because Philadelphia and Baltimore already have the most shift requests per AT, and new contracts draw on the same pool. Q4.2

  5. This board

    Reset the board expectation and fix the dashboard process

    Dashboard (owner: Analytics). Analytics owns the metric definitions and the reconciliation. An independent reviewer checks it, and the business owner signs off before publication.

    Board expectation (separate owner). All 8 remaining terms must renew to reach 70.4%; 7 would give 66.7%. This assumes the plan counts contract terms and the full-year cohort stays at 27. Confirm that definition before reporting against plan.

    Because three of eight board figures were wrong, and one more churn caps 2026 below plan. Part 1 · Q4.11B

  6. In parallel

    Investigate sales efficiency before reallocating time

    Check deal mix, qualification, sales effort and acquisition economics, and verify what the $518k tagged "no decision" represents. Two referral deals are too few to judge referral performance. Add Pittsburgh's $153k of January–March renewals to the renewal calendar with an owner and a review date.

    Because inbound won 7 of 12 closed opportunities against 2 of 31 outbound, and "no decision" is the costliest loss reason. Q4.8 · Q4.9 · Scorecard

Part 6 · How far to trust it

What I checked and what's still open

Validation

  • Scorecard totals match the corrected board figures: 19 due, 11 renewed, 8 churned; $2,321k book; 2,175 unfilled shifts; 79.26% fill.
  • Renewal exposure matches the investigation: 8 contracts ($615k) by December, 12 ($813k) by March.
  • Data checks pass: unique keys, statuses agree with the 09/30 snapshot, every renewed contract has a successor, every contract metro has marketplace data.
  • The book bridge from August to September reconciles with zero difference.
  • Each corrected figure can be rebuilt by hand in the spreadsheet with filters and sums: retention 11 of 19, fill 8,311 ÷ 10,486, win rate 11 of 45.
  • Queries parse as BigQuery SQL and were run on a local copy built from the pack.

Unresolved questions

  • Why did the 6 Philadelphia and Baltimore customers leave? Exit reasons decide whether fixing fill also fixes retention.
  • Were those customers' own contracted shifts covered? Fill is measured per metro, not per customer.
  • Why are ATs there not taking more shifts: availability, travel, pay, or a competitor? This needs AT-level data.
  • How are contract and marketplace shifts prioritized from the shared AT pool?
  • What would it cost to add 108–303 filled shifts a month, and how fast?
  • Does $32,750 per fill point hold for these two metros and at this scale?
  • Is D-1059 a duplicate of D-1056? Which figures does Finance's cash floor assume?
  • Is the 70% plan measured by count or value? By value, retention is 66.1%, still below plan.
