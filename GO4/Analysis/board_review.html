Part 2 · Why, and where?

The evidence chain

Each step tests one idea. Together they point to two metros, and they rule out the obvious alternatives.

  1. Fill fell in only two metros.

    Philadelphia went from 84% to 73% and Baltimore from 83% to 71% since April. Every other established metro improved by 2 to 3 points. Q4.1

  2. Those two metros hold half the problem.

    1,083 of the company's 2,175 unfilled shifts in September (50%) were in Philadelphia and Baltimore. Q4.3

  3. It isn't extra demand.

    Since April every metro got 44–54% more shift requests and added 22–35% more ATs. Elsewhere each AT took on more shifts (now 4.3–4.7 a month). In Philadelphia and Baltimore they stayed at about 4.2. Q4.2

  4. The same two metros are losing contract customers.

    Retention is 25% there (2 of 8) against 82% everywhere else (9 of 11). They account for 6 of the 8 lost contracts and $460k of the $500k churned value. Q4.5

  5. Ruled out: a segment problem, contract load, and sales as the cause.

    The lost customers there were 2 clubs, 2 schools and 2 facilities Q4.6. Charlotte has the most contracts per AT (4 per 100) and still fills 80% Q4.12. Outbound sales win only 2 of 31 deals against 7 of 12 inbound, which is a real but separate issue Q4.8.

  6. Caution: this shows the problems in the same place, not that one caused the other.

    Most customers left in June to August, before the September drop. In the three months before renewal, churned contracts there had about 81% fill against 83% for those that renewed. Charlotte had the lowest pre-renewal fill (78%) and kept both contracts. Q4.4

  7. More is at risk, and the 70% plan now needs every renewal.

    Eight contracts worth $615k renew by December, five of them ($387k) in Philadelphia and Baltimore Q4.7. Reaching 70% for 2026 takes all eight: 19 of 27 is 70.4%, and one more churn caps the year at 66.7% Q4.11B.

Part 4 · What to do

Recommendations

Concentrate the next 90 days on Philadelphia and Baltimore. Run a standard renewal play everywhere else.

  1. Next 60 days

    AT supply push in Philadelphia and Baltimore

    Recruit and reactivate ATs and put targeted incentives on hard-to-fill shifts. Target at least 75% fill by December, with 80% as the stretch. Track fill weekly by metro.

    Because demand grew everywhere, but only these two metros failed to raise shifts per AT (steps 1–3).

  2. Oct–Dec

    A named owner and coverage plan for the 5 renewals there ($387k)

    • Fairmount Park Athletics, Philadelphia · Oct · $34k
    • Inner Harbor Youth Sports, Baltimore · Oct · $29k
    • Penn Charter Network, Philadelphia · Nov · $92k
    • Navy Yard Fieldhouse, Philadelphia · Dec · $128k
    • Columbia Sports Park, Baltimore · Dec · $104k

    Because these metros renewed only 1 in 4 this year, and the two December contracts are 60% of the value (steps 4 and 7).

  3. Nov–Dec

    Early renewal contact for the other three

    Potomac Indoor Center, Washington DC ($112k), Charlotte Indoor Sports ($96k), Lehigh Valley Volleyball ($20k). Coverage is healthy in these metros, so this is account management, not extra coverage spend.

  4. Until fill recovers

    Hold new contract commitments in Philadelphia and Baltimore

    Resume once fill is back to 75% for two months running. New contracts draw on the same short AT pool.

  5. This board

    Reset the retention expectation and fix the dashboard process

    Tell the board that 70% for 2026 needs all 8 remaining renewals. Have someone outside the measured team calculate the dashboard, using the definitions in the reconciliation script.

  6. Later

    Shift sales time toward inbound and referrals

    Outbound wins 2 of 31. Worth fixing, but it won't change this quarter. Plan ahead for Pittsburgh's $153k renewing January to March.

Part 6 · How far to trust it

What I checked and what's still open

Validation

  • Scorecard totals match the corrected board figures: 19 due, 11 renewed, 8 churned; $2,321k book; 2,175 unfilled shifts; 79.26% fill.
  • Renewal exposure matches the investigation: 8 contracts ($615k) by December, 12 ($813k) by March.
  • Data checks pass: unique keys, statuses agree with the 09/30 snapshot, every renewed contract has a successor, every contract metro has marketplace data.
  • The book bridge from August to September reconciles with zero difference.
  • Each corrected figure can be rebuilt by hand in the spreadsheet with filters and sums: retention 11 of 19, fill 8,311 ÷ 10,486, win rate 11 of 45.
  • Queries parse as BigQuery SQL and were run on a local copy built from the pack.

Unresolved questions

  • Why did the 6 Philadelphia and Baltimore customers leave? Exit reasons decide whether fixing fill also fixes retention.
  • Were those customers' own contracted shifts covered? Fill is measured per metro, not per customer.
  • Why are ATs there not taking more shifts: availability, travel, pay, or a competitor? This needs AT-level data.
  • How are contract and marketplace shifts prioritized from the shared AT pool?
  • What would it cost to add 108–303 filled shifts a month, and how fast?
  • Does $32,750 per fill point hold for these two metros and at this scale?
  • Is D-1059 a duplicate of D-1056? Which figures does Finance's cash floor assume?
  • Is the 70% plan measured by count or value? By value, retention is 66.1%, still below plan.
